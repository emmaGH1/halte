## D1–D6 verdicts

### D1 · Accept PQ-signed offers as MUST, with separate nonces

Inside an already-open window, the current `makeOffer` relies entirely on the holder's EVM sender address. The existing recipient key check applies to the recipient, so it cannot establish the holder's consent. A fresh ML-DSA-87 signature for the exact dealer, passport and expiry closes that gap. Use the current holder's PoP-backed registry key and verify the raw 32-byte digest. A relayer who copies the signed intent can submit the exact offer first. The dealer address is signed and acceptance still requires that dealer as sender, so neither a copied signature nor a competing dealer can redirect it. The client should reconcile against the confirmed offer event and the signed calldata if submission loses that race. A named dealer can still accept before a freeze confirms. An offer also grants the named dealer discretion to accept until it expires, is withdrawn, or its epoch becomes stale. Keep classical cancellation and document that it permits withdrawal griefing during a live window. [Current contract](../../contracts/contracts/Halte.sol#L68) · [QoreChain window reference](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-guide/evm-window-reference/) · [PQC precompiles](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-guide/evm-precompiles/)

Use a separate offer nonce and sign the epoch. Mixing offer creation into the lifecycle nonce lets a pending, valid offer land first and invalidate a pending freeze even though the holder has not changed. With the split, an offer first leaves the freeze valid, and a freeze first invalidates the pending offer. Acceptance first still invalidates the prior holder's freeze through the ownership and lifecycle nonce changes. Simultaneously prepared offers at the same offer nonce are mutually exclusive. Budget for ABI, signing, release and tests as well as the Solidity diff. The verification precompile alone is documented at roughly 85–90k gas for typical ML-DSA-87 inputs, plus the transaction's calldata and application work. Measure on Diana before setting caps. [Lifecycle digest code](../../contracts/contracts/Halte.sol#L104) · [PQC verification gas](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-guide/evm-precompiles/#pqc-verify-verify-a-post-quantum-signature)

### D2 · Reject a successful no-op in `acceptOffer` as MUST

A transaction with status success and no transfer forces every caller, indexer and demo client to control for the missing `Transferred` event. The proposed emit-only path lets the named dealer repeat the same event, and an early frozen check also covers offers already stale before this freeze and offers that have expired. The dealer pays for the writes, including the event log, and spends their window allowance. Halte pays both costs if its public signer submits them. Consuming the offer prevents repetition but erases the distinction between `PassportFrozen` and `StaleOffer` in the same-offer demo. An event can establish which on-chain account supplied the call. It gives no evidence of a person, a physical watch or an attempted purchase. For this entry an identical, pinned positive/negative call sequence plus the actual freeze and recovery receipts explains the security property more directly. [Current acceptance checks](../../contracts/contracts/Halte.sol#L78) · [Track requirements](https://build.qorechain.io/prizes#quantum_safe_dapp)

The semantics I would accept for a later opt-in feature are `function recordBlockedAttempt(uint256 offerId) external` and `event BlockedAcceptanceReported(bytes32 indexed id, uint256 indexed offerId, address indexed dealer)`. Require an open offer (`OfferClosed`), its named sender (`NotDealer`), a frozen passport (`NotFrozen`), the epoch immediately preceding that freeze (`StaleOffer`), an unexpired offer (`Expired`) and the dealer's usable PoP-backed key (`NoPqcKey`). Store one bit per reported offer and revert with `error BlockedAttemptAlreadyReported();` on repeat. Leave the offer and passport untouched and use receipt metadata for height. The dealer broadcasts and pays. Label it “Dealer account recorded a blocked acceptance” and keep `acceptOffer` reverting. This is a STRETCH design for after the freeze.

### D3 · Compromise on the tagline

Use **“Reported missing. Passport transfers blocked.”** alongside “Demo transfer passport” and a fictional watch reference. Keep the stopped movement and the red stamp. Apply the scoped wording to the hero, share metadata, certificate, README and film, where judges may encounter the claim in isolation. Explain that a cooperating dealer has to check the genuine passport. The demo shows transfer authorization for a record created by a caller-supplied ID. The product distinction is PQ authorization and permanent invalidation of pre-freeze offers across recovery. ### D4 · Reject pending nonce plus retry as the write coordinator

Two Vercel instances can read the same pending counter and both send. RPC backends can have different mempool views, and `already known` may be confirmation that the exact bytes are pending. A timeout can happen after admission or inclusion. Retrying the logical action with a fresh nonce can mint a second passport or issue a second offer if the server also regenerates the ID or PQ intent. The window path makes this worse. Two instances can renew at once, or one can renew while another is preparing an EVM write. A native window opening replaces the existing window and advances **the same counter** the EVM write uses. An in-process queue outlives neither a cold start nor a concurrent instance. A process-local per-IP or global budget has the same weakness. Use one cross-instance writer per account with a durable operation record. A fresh pending nonce is an input after reconciliation. [Window and write code](../../web/lib/chain.ts#L81) · [QoreChain window reference](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-guide/evm-window-reference/) · [QoreChain custody guidance](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-tools/custody-kit/)

### D5 · Accept a 60-second core with an optional evidence tail

Deliver a captioned, logged-out-playable proof film within the three-minute limit. Make the first minute self-contained. Extend to roughly 90–120 seconds if the second segment shows the actual scoped window, hybrid event and freeze calldata/receipt. A short problem setup belongs in the opening shot. The same offer, same dealer and unchanged owner do the explanatory work. [Submission rules](https://build.qorechain.io/rules)

### D6 · Accept the dealer account as demo relayer

Use the demo owner to sign, the dealer account with its own scoped window to submit freeze and recover, and preserve permissionless relay in Solidity. Pin the sender, holder and registry evidence. The dealer needs PoP for the credible acceptance control and funding for actual broadcasts. Both demo roles are derived by the same service today, so differing addresses demonstrate the contract relay path, while the custody disclosure remains “Halte holds the demo signing keys.” A separately held SDK signing key is a STRETCH. QoreX already supports `signArbitrary`, but its documented prefix changes the bytes passed to `pqcVerify`. Direct compatibility with Halte's raw digest requires a separate integration and proof. [Demo account derivation](../../web/lib/chain.ts#L53) · [QoreX message signing](https://build.qorechain.io/faq#qorex)

### 2 · MUST · Release one contract with these semantics

- Add `uint64 offerNonce` at the end of `Passport`, initially zero. Keep `nonce` as the lifecycle counter. Keep the existing mint, freeze, recover, cancel, accept and status function signatures. Change issuance to `function makeOffer(bytes32 id, address dealer, uint64 expiry, bytes calldata signature) external returns (uint256 offerId)`. Add `function offerDigest(bytes32 id, address dealer, uint64 epoch, uint64 offerNonce, uint64 expiry) public view returns (bytes32)`, returning `keccak256(abi.encode("HALTE_OFFER_V1", block.chainid, address(this), id, dealer, epoch, offerNonce, expiry))`. The signer signs the digest bytes directly. The contract derives epoch and nonce from storage at execution.
- For issuance, check `UnknownPassport()`, `PassportFrozen()`, and new `error InvalidDealer();` for zero or the current holder, in that order. Reject `block.timestamp > expiry` with `Expired()`, then verify against the current holder's key. Allow any EVM-admitted sender to relay. Increment only `offerNonce` on successful issuance. Append `uint64 expiry` to `Offer` and emit `event OfferMade(uint256 indexed offerId, bytes32 indexed id, address indexed dealer, uint64 epoch, uint64 expiry);`. Add no caller-selected dealer or arbitrary calldata to the public signing API.
- For acceptance, check `OfferClosed()`, `NotDealer()`, `PassportFrozen()`, `StaleOffer()`, `Expired()`, then recipient key admission, in that order. Equality with expiry is valid. Delete the accepted offer, change owner, increment epoch and lifecycle nonce, and emit the existing `Transferred(bytes32 indexed id, address indexed from, address indexed to, uint256 offerId)`. Freeze and recovery each increment epoch and lifecycle nonce as today. Leave the existing `Frozen(bytes32 indexed id, address indexed owner, uint64 epoch, uint64 nonce)`, `Recovered(bytes32 indexed id, address indexed owner, uint64 epoch, uint64 nonce)` and `OfferCancelled(uint256 indexed offerId, bytes32 indexed id)` events intact. Cancellation stays holder-only and deletes one offer. Epoch changes invalidate siblings without scanning storage. There is no `SaleBlocked` event in this release.
- Decode `pqcKeyStatus(address) external view returns (bool registered, uint8 algorithmId, bytes memory pubkey, bool possessionProven)`. For mint, acceptance and every signed action require registered, algorithm 1, 2,592-byte key and PoP. Use `NoPqcKey()` for well-formed but unusable key records and `PrecompileUnavailable()` for failed, empty or malformed precompile responses. Check ABI head, bools, offset and length bounds before decoding the dynamic registry return, and require a canonical 32-byte bool from the verifier. In `_checkPq`, enforce expiry, reject signatures other than 4,627 bytes with `BadPqcSignature()`, then load the key and verify. A canonical false result is also `BadPqcSignature()`. [Published precompile ABI](https://docs.qorechain.io/developer-guide/evm-precompiles/)
- Regenerate the web ABI and address/height manifest together. This is a fresh deployment. Keep historical links keyed by deployment and avoid reading an old record with the new ABI or implying old passports moved across automatically. ## Remaining disagreements

- **D1 effort.** Claude estimates 3–4 incremental hours for offer PQ signing. The lower estimate fits the contract mechanics. Separate nonce handling, web ABI/signing and cross-layer release proof still go on the critical path. Both positions support closing the consent gap for the final pitch.
- **D2 product claim.** Claude favors a dealer-funded, non-reverting receipt inside acceptance as the memorable hook. This plan preserves reverting acceptance and uses paired calls. An explicit dealer report remains a later product decision.
- **D3 copy.** Claude would keep “Stolen means unsellable” with a precise lede. This plan scopes the headline itself.
- **D4 availability.** Claude favors bounded retries, a cookie and shared caps without durable serialization. This plan gates public writes on cross-instance sequencing and idempotency. The recorded proof covers the read-only fallback.

## Round 3 · Claude's final position (23 Nov)

- **D1:** agreed as written, including the separate `offerNonce` and the signed epoch. Codex's split-nonce argument is right, and it closes a front-running case my version had.
- **D2:** conceded. Codex is right that a successful-but-no-transfer acceptance is a semantic trap. Its spam and stale-offer cases are real, and a same-offer positive/negative control proves more. `SaleBlocked` is dropped. `recordBlockedAttempt` stays a post-freeze idea.
- **D3:** the human decides. Option A is Codex's: the scoped headline "Reported missing. Passport transfers blocked." Option B is mine: keep "Stolen means unsellable." as the brand line, with the scoped sentence directly under it everywhere the claim appears alone. Both sides agree on "Demo transfer passport" and a fictional reference.
- **D4:** partly conceded. Codex is right that a native window renewal and an EVM write share one sequence counter, so a retry alone can double-mint or race a renewal. Agreed middle ground:
  - one shared Redis (Upstash via the Vercel integration) per-signer lock (`SET NX PX`), held across window check, renewal, sign and receipt;
  - a client idempotency key mapped to the operation result;
  - shared rate limits and daily caps in the same Redis;
  - public writes disabled (read-only plus the recorded run) whenever Redis is unreachable.
  
  The full journal that rebroadcasts exact bytes is SHOULD, not MUST. With one writer per signer and a stored idempotency result, the remaining gap is a function dying between broadcast and receipt. The lock's TTL plus a reconcile-by-nonce on the next acquire covers that.
- **D5, D6:** agreed.
